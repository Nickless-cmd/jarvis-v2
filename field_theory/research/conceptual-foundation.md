# Conceptual foundation

**Status:** initial research framing, 2026-10-30. This document states starting
propositions precisely enough to investigate them without presenting them as
scientific findings.

## Neutral restatement

Feltteori begins with the possibility that what an observer encounters is
determined not only by isolated information, but by access, perspective,
context, and relations among observations. Apparent randomness may sometimes
be structure that is unavailable under a current representation, while in
other cases it may be stochastic variation, noise, bias, or an artefact of the
method. Persistent relations and their transformations may contribute to
continuity and identity. Memory and expectation are present organizations of
information about what is called past and future; this raises the question of
whether some time-like organization can be described relationally rather than
by clock time alone. Experience may alter context or future response without
becoming an explicit learned rule.

This restatement makes no claim that a universal physical field exists, that
all information is accessible, that every random event is predictable, that a
computational representation has subjective experience, or that physical time
is derived from perspective.

## Foundational propositions

1. Information may exist independently of one observer's present ability to
   access or interpret it.
2. Existence, availability, accessibility, observation, and interpretation of
   information are different relations and must not be collapsed.
3. A process that appears random from one perspective may exhibit structure
   from another; this does not exclude genuinely stochastic processes.
4. Observable interpretation depends on available information, context,
   prior relations, distinctions, and assumptions.
5. Relations and their continuity may be more explanatory than isolated
   observations, and chronology is only one possible relation.
6. Memory and expectation occur as present states. Expectation is not access
   to, or knowledge of, a future event.
7. Experience and learning differ: an encounter may change context,
   expectation, or response without producing an explicit rule or improved
   prediction.
8. The propositions themselves are revisable. The project remains useful if
   any of them are contradicted or dissolved by better definitions.

## Ambiguities that must remain visible

### Information and field

“Information exists” may mean a physical state has distinguishable structure,
a description is logically possible, a record persists, or an observer could
in principle recover a distinction. These meanings have different empirical
commitments. “The field is constant while expanding” is presently ambiguous:
constant could refer to identity, rules, capacity, or presence; expanding could
refer to content, accessible relations, extent, or an observer's model. Until
an interpretation yields discriminating observations, *field* is a conceptual
placeholder and must not be equated with a physical field.

### Randomness and pattern

“Randomness is a pattern not yet mapped” can be read as a methodological
invitation (search under alternative representations), an epistemic claim
(randomness describes observer knowledge), or an ontological claim (no genuine
randomness exists). Only the first is assumed here. Finite data can always
support accidental or overfit descriptions.

### Perspective and observer

Perspective could mean restricted access, a coordinate transform, selected
features, prior experience, a model class, an interpretive convention, or a
subjective point of view. Computational experiments can manipulate the first
five. They cannot by themselves establish subjective experience.

### Relations, continuity, and identity

Relations may be temporal, causal, statistical, semantic, spatial,
structural, or contextual. Continuity may mean persistence of components,
relations, constraints, function, narrative, or recognition across change.
“Identity emerges” needs a criterion for when two states count as the same
identity; no such universal criterion is assumed.

### Time and three experiences of now

The “three experiences of now” formulation needs further elicitation from
Bjørn before formalization. It must not silently be translated into ordinary
past/present/future categories. Physical time, clock labels, event order,
perceived duration, memory, expectation, and present interpretation remain
separate concepts.

### Experience and learning

Experience may denote an encounter, a changed internal context, or subjective
phenomenology. Learning may denote durable model change, explicit knowledge,
or improved behavior. Operational definitions will be local to an experiment,
not universal replacements for the concepts.

## Potential observable consequences

These are conditional research possibilities, not predictions of the whole
theory:

- The same preserved records may show different dependencies when access to
  contextual relations changes.
- A relationship found under one representation may disappear under a suitable
  control, permutation, or out-of-sample replication.
- Observation order may change a later representation or expectation even when
  the multiset of observations is unchanged.
- A representation based on relational transitions may retain predictive
  structure when explicit timestamps are removed.
- New observations may alter some expectations without improving a specified
  prediction, offering an operational distinction between experience and
  learning.
- Competing continuity criteria may disagree about whether identity persisted
  through the same transformation.

Each consequence requires a local model of observer, access, relation, and
measurement. Evidence for one would support only that narrow formulation.

## Competing explanations to carry forward

Any detected structure should be compared, where relevant, with stochastic
variation, measurement noise, pseudorandom-generator properties, sampling and
selection bias, data leakage, multiple comparisons, flexible stopping,
overfitting, transformations that manufacture dependence, common causes,
hidden variables, chronology, and correlations without causal force. An
observer-dependent result may be a consequence of feature selection rather
than evidence about subjective perspective. Failure to detect structure may
reflect low power or an unsuitable representation, but is not evidence that a
hidden meaningful structure must exist.

## Questions not currently testable as stated

- Does information exist with no actual or possible physical distinction or
  observer relation?
- Is there a universal field that is simultaneously constant and expanding?
- Does perspective ontologically precede physical time?
- Does any computational representation possess subjective experience?
- Is all apparent randomness unrecognized deterministic structure?
- Does relational continuity constitute identity in every relevant sense?

These may become tractable after refinement, or remain philosophical. The
notebook should record refinements rather than treating inability to test as
support or contradiction.

## Research discipline

Raw record, measurement, and interpretation occupy separate layers. An
exploratory anomaly is a prompt for a new question. A confirmatory claim needs
a method and evaluation rule recorded before its confirming data are examined.
Methods may change, but old data, code version, parameters, derived output, and
reasons for revision remain recoverable. There is no universal theory score.
